The film builds one landscape the way graphics has built landscapes for forty years. It starts
with a lattice of random gradients, then noise, octaves stacked at half the amplitude and twice
the frequency, a heightfield that rises out of the grey image, clouds from a second fbm, and a sun.
Each step is a rule that someone wrote down and that you can read.

Then a foreign signal cuts into the archive feed. It does not show a better picture. It shows a
different way of thinking about the same picture: mountains as samples from a distribution, light
as a guess from a cache, a reflectance formula as a weight matrix, geometry as the minimum of a loss.
The landscape stays the same set for all four chapters. Only the reasoning behind it changes.

|---|---|---|
| Noise | Ken Perlin, *An Image Synthesizer*, SIGGRAPH 1985 | Ho, Jain & Abbeel, *Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models*, NeurIPS 2020; Song et al., *Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations*, ICLR 2021 |
| Light | James T. Kajiya, *The Rendering Equation*, SIGGRAPH 1986 | Müller, Rousselle, Novák & Keller, *Real-time Neural Radiance Caching for Path Tracing*, ACM TOG 40(4), SIGGRAPH 2021 |
| Material | Cook & Torrance, *A Reflectance Model for Computer Graphics*, SIGGRAPH 1981 | Zeltner et al., *Real-Time Neural Appearance Models*, ACM TOG 43(3), 2024 |
| Geometry | Karis, Stubbe & Wihlidal, *Nanite: A Deep Dive*, SIGGRAPH 2021 Advances in Real-Time Rendering course | Kerbl, Kopanas, Leimkühler & Drettakis, *3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering*, ACM TOG 42(4), SIGGRAPH 2023 |

## What the interruptions show

- **Noise.** Samples from $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ follow the score $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ until they
  settle into the ridge line of our own terrain. Stochastic reverse-time trajectories fan out and
  converge onto a three-mode density. Then the landscape comes back as a $64\times64\times4$ latent that
  starts as static and is fitted step by step. It ends as almost the same mountains, but the fine
  detail is a different sample.
- **Light.** A camera path is cut at its first hit. That vertex becomes the input of a small network
  that is trained while it renders. Back in the valley, every path now stops at a hot query node and
  reports to the same network. Only a few paths continue, to train the cache. (The real method
  traces short paths and uses a heuristic to pick where to stop. The film always cuts at the first
  hit.)
- **Material.** The symbols of $R_s = \frac{F}{\pi}\frac{DG}{(N\cdot L)(N\cdot V)}$ come apart into
  neurons, and a $64\times64$ weight matrix keeps changing. The Beckmann lobe on the rock becomes a
  lumpy learned lobe that never settles.
- **Geometry.** The heightfield is read as a loss surface, with optimisers rolling down $-\nabla L$
  into its valleys. Then the triangles are gone. Gaussians spawn from one isotropic blob,
  descend onto the land, get cloned, split and pruned, and a few floaters stay in the sky.

## Why this is the unsettling part

The pictures a learned model makes are not the frightening part; the classical methods make
pictures too. What changes is where the knowledge lives. Perlin's octave sum, Kajiya's integral
and Cook and Torrance's microfacet term are claims about the world, and you can argue with them.
A score network, a radiance cache and a neural BRDF decoder hold the same knowledge as fitted
numbers. They answer correctly without saying why.

The honest version is less dramatic. Every method in the right-hand column was built by graphics
researchers, trained on data or renders produced with the left-hand column, and judged against it.
The radiance cache is trained on path-traced radiance. Neural materials are fitted to
reference layered materials. Splats are optimised through a rasteriser against photographs. The formulas did not
disappear. They moved into the training loop, where fewer people read them.

## How the film is made

Everything is procedural and rendered frame by frame with WebGL2 in headless Chromium. **The AI
shots are visualisations of the ideas, not outputs of trained models.** No network is trained or
evaluated anywhere in the film.

What is computed for real:

- **Terrain.** Six octaves of 2D gradient noise: pseudo-random unit gradients on a periodic
  lattice with Perlin's later quintic fade. The lattice spacing goes from 4 world units down to
  1/8, and each octave is summed at amplitude $2^{-i}$. The octaves are computed on the CPU into a
  float texture and ray marched in a fragment shader. The clouds are a second, thresholded fbm
  marched through a slab.
- **Light paths.** 34 paths ray cast against the same heightfield, with cosine-weighted bounces
  and shadow rays towards the sun. The image noise that falls as $1/\sqrt{\text{spp}}$ is simulated.
- **Reflectance.** The rock's highlight and the wire lobe use the 1981 form above with a Beckmann
  $D$ and the V-groove $G$. Fresnel uses Schlick's approximation instead of the full equations.
- **Geometry view.** A world-space triangle grid with clusters of 128 triangles and a level of
  detail that doubles with distance. It imitates the look of Nanite's visualisation, not its
  cluster DAG or error metric.
- **Sampling shots.** DDPM ancestral sampling with a linear $\beta$ schedule, strided to 44 and
  120 steps. The score is the
  *exact* score of a hand-made toy density (a blurred silhouette of the terrain's ridge line, and a
  1D three-component mixture), not a learned $s_\theta$.
- **Loss surface.** The optimiser trails are gradient descent with momentum on the heightfield.
- **Splats.** Each Gaussian's 3D covariance is projected with the Jacobian of the perspective map
  (EWA, as in 3D Gaussian Splatting), and the splats are sorted and alpha-blended.

What is illustrative:

- The latent follows a fixed schedule towards the terrain's cell means.
- The cache's error is scripted to shrink.
- The "MLP" on the rock has eight units with random weights that drift.
- The weight matrices are made from sinusoids.
- The splats move to precomputed positions on the terrain, taking their colours from the beauty
  render. No loss is evaluated.

The soundtrack is synthesised in numpy from the same event list as the picture. Each octave of
noise adds a musical octave. In the sampling shots, white noise is filtered into a chord. The
optimisers glide downwards in pitch. The source is in the site's repository under `studio/note01`.
