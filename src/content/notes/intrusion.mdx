For fifty years, computer graphics wrote things down. Noise got a formula. Light got an integral.
Materials got a microfacet distribution. Geometry got an index buffer. Each of these was a
statement about the world that you could read, prove and debug.

In the last decade, each of them acquired a learned twin that skips the statement and goes straight
to the picture. The film plays four of these pairs. Each chapter starts as a calm archive recording
of the classical technique, then the counterpart breaks in.

| Chapter | Written down | Learned |
|---|---|---|
| Texture | Perlin, *An Image Synthesizer*, SIGGRAPH 1985 | Ho, Jain & Abbeel, *Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models*, NeurIPS 2020 |
| Light transport | Kajiya, *The Rendering Equation*, SIGGRAPH 1986 | Bako et al., *Kernel-Predicting Convolutional Networks for Denoising Monte Carlo Renderings*, SIGGRAPH 2017 |
| Materials | Cook & Torrance, *A Reflectance Model for Computer Graphics*, SIGGRAPH 1981 | Zeltner et al., *Real-Time Neural Appearance Models*, ACM TOG 2024 |
| Geometry | Karis, Stubbe & Wihlidal, *Nanite: A Deep Dive*, SIGGRAPH 2021 course | Kerbl et al., *3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering*, SIGGRAPH 2023 |

## Why horror

The irony that interests me is in the first chapter. Procedural noise was graphics' way of faking
nature's detail on purpose. Diffusion models are trained to *remove* noise, and they turn out to
make pictures by running that removal backwards from pure static. The same noise that Perlin
tamed is now the raw material.

The honest version is less dramatic: every learned counterpart in the table was built by graphics
researchers, trained on renders made with the classical method, and is judged against it. The
denoiser needs the path tracer. The splats are optimised against photographs through a
rasteriser. The formula is not gone; it moved into the training loop. But the part you can read is
shrinking, and that is worth a minute of unease.

## How the film is made

Everything is procedural and rendered in the browser:

- **Perlin marble**: 3D gradient noise with Perlin's quintic fade, turbulence driving $\sin(x + k\,\mathrm{turb}(\mathbf{p}))$. The diffusion chapter applies the forward process $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$ to the frame, then "reverses" to a different $x_0$.
- **Cornell box**: a real Monte Carlo path tracer with next-event estimation, shown at 1 to 8 samples per pixel. The "denoiser" is an edge-aware filter, not a network; its over-smoothing is deliberate.
- **BRDF chart**: GGX distribution, Smith geometry term and Schlick Fresnel at five roughness values. The neural phase replaces the formula with a tiny random MLP over a coarse latent grid.
- **Cliffs**: a ray-marched height field, then a cluster view whose cluster size doubles with distance, the way Nanite's LOD visualisation looks. The splat phase re-draws the frame as oriented screen-space Gaussians, with floaters.
- **Sound**: synthesised from the same event list as the picture, so every glitch and keystroke lines up.
